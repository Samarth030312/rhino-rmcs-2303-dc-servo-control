## 1. Direction fixed

## 2. Open loop (MOTOR-02, 5 runs, 4 usable)
- Steady speed equals the command within ±2 rpm at 20/40/80/120 rpm. The drive's
  internal loop has no steady-state error.
- The 10-90 % rise time grows with step size: 0.2-0.3 s for 20 rpm steps, 0.6-0.7 s
  for 40 rpm steps. That is a slope of ≈ 50 output rpm/s, which matches the
  acceleration register (5000 base rpm/s ÷ 100). The drive is rate-limited, not
  first-order.
- The speed feedback is coarse: it moves in steps of about **4.5-4.65 output rpm**
  (450-465 base rpm). One step on a 20 rpm setpoint looks like a 20-25 % "overshoot".
  This comes from the feedback resolution, not from the dynamics.

## 3. Speed P sweep (MOTOR-05), setpoint 60 rpm
The drive tracks the command, so with P alone y = Kp·(r − y), which gives
y_ss = Kp/(1+Kp)·r.

| Kp | predicted y_ss | measured y_ss | e_ss measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 | 10.0 | 10.5 | 49.5 |
| 0.5 | 20.0 | 19.8 | 40.2 |
| 0.8 | 26.7 | 26.8 | 33.2 |

Measured and predicted agree to within one feedback step. P control alone leaves a
large steady-state error that falls as Kp rises.

## 4. Speed PI (MOTOR-03 × 4 runs, MOTOR-07 × 3 runs), Kp 0.3, Ki 1.5
- Zero steady-state error (|e_ss| ≤ 0.8 rpm) at 50/100/30/0 rpm and at 60 rpm.
- Overshoot 3-12 % on rising steps. The 100 → 30 rpm step shows 17 % (≈ 12 rpm,
  under 3 feedback steps). Settling time is 0.8-1.3 s, or 1.9-2.2 s for the 100 → 30 step.
- Runs are repeatable: the four MOTOR-03 runs agree within one feedback step.

## 5. Position P (MOTOR-04 × 2, MOTOR-05 sweep), Kp in 1/s
- Zero steady-state error (≤ 0.4°, i.e. ≤ 6 counts) for ±90° and 360°. The
  plant integrates speed to angle, so P alone is enough.
- 90° step: Kp 0.2 gives no overshoot (t_rise 1.65 s), Kp 0.3 gives 1 % (0.9 s),
  and Kp 0.5 gives 20 % (0.6 s). Higher gain is faster but overshoots more,
  because the drive's 50 rpm/s ramp acts like extra lag in the loop.
- 360° move with Kp 0.3: 21-23 % overshoot. The command saturates near 135 rpm,
  and the ramp-limited drive cannot slow down in time. The overshoot depends on
  step size, so this is a nonlinear effect. Linear analysis of the outer loop
  holds only for small steps.

## 6. Position I only (MOTOR-06, Ki 0.1)
The angle oscillates around 45° (peak ≈ 93°, trough ≈ −6°, period ≈ 8 s) and
does not settle in 12 s. This is the expected behaviour of two integrators in the
loop.
